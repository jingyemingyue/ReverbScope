## B. Methodology notes

### B.1 ISO 3382-1 / 3382-2 evaluation of the decay curve

*Sources:* ISO 3382-1:2009 §3 (definitions) and the integrated-impulse-response annex; ISO 3382-2:2008 §3 and §5; quoted verbatim-in-substance by Hak et al. 2012 (A.5/17a) and Christensen et al. 2013 (A.5/17d); TN007 "Impulse response to noise ratio" (Acoustics Engineering, https://www.acoustics-engineering.com/files/TN007.pdf).

- **Decay curve:** L(t) = 10·log10( ∫_t^∞ p²(τ)dτ / ∫_0^∞ p²(τ)dτ ) — Schroeder backward integral, normalised to 0 dB at t = 0 (onset of direct sound).
- **Evaluation ranges** (linear least-squares regression line fitted to L(t) over the range; slope d in dB/s; T = 60/d):
  - **EDT:** 0 dB to −10 dB (extrapolated ×6 to 60 dB).
  - **T20:** −5 dB to −25 dB (time for 20 dB ×3).
  - **T30:** −5 dB to −35 dB (time for 30 dB ×2).
  - The start at −5 dB is to exclude the direct sound and strong early reflections (Hak et al. 2012 restating ISO 3382).
- **Noise margin:** the lower limit of the evaluation range (−25 dB for T20, −35 dB for T30) shall be **at least 10 dB above the background-noise level** of the decay curve (ISO 3382-1/-2; restated in Hak et al. 2012 and in Karjalainen et al. 2002: "according to the ISO 3382 standard the noise floor must be 10 dB below the lowest decay level used").
- **Required decay range ("insufficient dynamic range" in practice):** ISO 3382 therefore requires a decay range of **≥ 35 dB for T20** and **≥ 45 dB for T30** (5 + 20 + 10 and 5 + 30 + 10 dB). Hak et al. 2012 show the peak-to-noise ratio (PNR) overstates the usable range; they define **INR = L_IR − L_N** (level of the backward-integrated curve's start vs. noise level) and recommend **INR ≥ 35 dB for T20, ≥ 45 dB for T30** (plus their Table of INR needed per parameter). RoomScope should compute INR per band and mark T20/T30 "not evaluable" below these limits; report EDT only if the curve is ≥ 20 dB above noise (their EDT threshold).
- **Fit quality:** ISO 3382 specifies least-squares regression; a non-linearity (curvature) indicator and the correlation coefficient may be reported (ISO 3382-1 lists "degree of non-linearity" and "degree of curvature" as optional outputs). *Not verified from the standard text itself:* vendor pages quote r² ≥ 0.995/0.997 thresholds — treat these as software conventions, not ISO requirements.
- **Octave bands** (ISO 3382-2): at least 125 Hz–4 kHz octave bands (63 Hz–8 kHz optional), 1/3-octave optional; filters per IEC 61260-1 class 1; mid-band frequencies base-10.
- **Averaging:** ISO 3382-2 accuracy levels — survey (≥ 1 source × 3 mic positions ... engineering ≥ 2 × 6 ... precision), average T over positions (spatial average of T values, not of decay curves) — recommend RoomScope offers "survey" as default for a single position with a clear label.

### B.2 Schroeder integration and Lundeby noise handling

*Sources:* Schroeder 1965 (A.1/4); Lundeby et al. 1995 (A.1/5) as summarised step-by-step in Karjalainen et al. 2002 (A.3/15a, Sec. 2.5, which cites Lundeby as ref. [8]); Christensen et al. 2013 (compensation constant); Guski & Vorländer 2014.

- **Schroeder (1965):** ⟨s²(t)⟩ = N ∫_t^∞ r²(x)dx — the ensemble average of squared interrupted-noise decays equals the backward integral of the squared impulse response. Implementation: E(t) = ∑_{k≥t} h²[k]; L(t) = 10 log10(E(t)/E(0)).
- **Truncation and compensation (Christensen et al. 2013, eq. (3)):** with a finite IR truncated at t1, E(t) = ∫_t^{t1} h²dτ + C, where C is the estimated energy beyond t1 obtained by extrapolating the late-decay regression line to infinity (C = A·exp(−2·6.9·t1/T_late)/(2·6.9/T_late) form, i.e. exponential tail integral). Without C the RT reads too short; without truncation it reads too long.
- **Lundeby et al. iterative algorithm** (as published in Karjalainen et al. 2002; numeric parameters are theirs):
  1. Average the squared IR in local time intervals of **10–50 ms** (smooth without losing short decays).
  2. First background-noise estimate from the **last 10 %** of the IR.
  3. Estimate the decay slope by linear regression between the interval containing the 0 dB peak and the first interval **5–10 dB above** the noise level.
  4. Preliminary cross-point = intersection of the decay line and the noise level.
  5. Choose a new interval length from the slope so that there are **3–10 intervals per 10 dB** of decay.
  6. Re-average the squared IR with the new intervals.
  7. Re-estimate the noise level from a segment starting **5–10 dB of decay after the cross-point**, but at least **10 % of the response length**.
  8. Re-estimate the late-decay slope over a **10–20 dB** range starting **5–10 dB above** the noise level.
  9. Find a new cross-point. **Iterate steps 7–9 until the cross-point converges (max. 5 iterations).**
  10. Truncate the IR at the cross-point and add the compensation energy C from the extrapolated late-decay line (as in the previous bullet).
- Alternative/complementary: **Chu's noise subtraction** — subtract the mean-square noise estimate from h² before integrating (Chu 1978, restated by Karjalainen 2002; compared in Guski & Vorländer 2014). ODEON (Christensen 2013) and pyrato's documented Chu–Lundeby curve do both truncation and subtraction. RoomScope: Lundeby truncation + compensation remains the noise-floor estimator; Chu subtraction is then applied to `h²` before the integral when a floor is found (`AnalysisSettings.decay_subtract_noise`, default on). Clean-room implementation; no third-party source copied.

### B.3 Filter bandwidth × reverberation-time limits (B·T)

*Sources:* Jacobsen 1987 "A note on acoustic decay measurements" (B·T ≥ 16 for a band-pass filter applied normally); Jacobsen & Rindel 1987 "Time reversed decay measurements" (B·T ≳ 4 with time-reversed filtering). Confirmed through secondary sources: Cabrera et al. 2016; the Applied Acoustics 2022 paper "Incremental use of FFT as a solution for low BT-product reverberation time measurements" (states "the BT = 4 line corresponds to the fastest performance of the time reversed method of Jacobsen and Rindel"); and B&K / ISO 3382 commentary that the decay curve is straight "as long as BT is larger than 16". **The primary JSV texts were not accessible online, so the numbers 16 and 4 are confirmed only via these citing works.**
- Practical rule for RoomScope: with IEC 61260-1 octave filters (B ≈ 0.707·fc), require T ≥ 16/B for straightforward filtering; at 63 Hz (B ≈ 44 Hz) that is T ≥ 0.36 s, at 125 Hz T ≥ 0.18 s. Recording rooms often have T < 0.3 s, so **filter the IR time-reversed** (filter the reversed IR, reverse back), which relaxes the rule to T ≥ 4/B (≈ 0.09 s at 63 Hz). Flag bands where B·T < 4 as unreliable.

### B.4 Farina exponential sine sweep — formulas (verified against the 2000 preprint, Sec. 3, and the 2007 paper, eq. (1))

- **Sweep:** x(t) = sin[ (ω1·T / ln(ω2/ω1)) · ( exp( (t/T)·ln(ω2/ω1) ) − 1 ) ], 0 ≤ t ≤ T, with ω = 2πf. Identical to the requested form x(t) = sin[(2π f1 T/ln(f2/f1))·(exp(t·ln(f2/f1)/T) − 1)]. (Farina derives it from x(t) = sin[K(e^{t/L} − 1)] with K = T·ω1/ln(ω2/ω1), L = T/ln(ω2/ω1).)
- **Inverse filter (2000, Sec. 5):** "time-reversing the excitation signal, and then applying to it an amplitude envelope to reduce the level by 6 dB/octave, starting from 0 dB and ending to −6·log2(ω2/ω1)" dB. Equivalently f(t) = x(T − t)·exp(−t·ln(ω2/ω1)/T) (a −6 dB/oct amplitude decay along the reversed sweep). The 6 dB/oct (amplitude) compensates the −3 dB/oct energy (pink) spectrum of the log sweep (Müller & Massarani, Sec. 4) so that x * f ≈ δ. Linear convolution y * f then yields h(t) with a fixed delay equal to the inverse-filter length.
- **Harmonic distortion products (2000, Sec. 3):** the N-th order harmonic response packs at a constant lead **Δt = T·ln(N)/ln(ω2/ω1)** *before* the linear response ("each harmonic order will pack always at a very precise time lag before the linear response"; spacing shrinks with order). Müller & Massarani independently: distortion products appear "at negative times where they can be separated completely from the actual IR"; with FFT (circular) deconvolution they wrap to the end of the buffer unless the sweep is much longer than the IR or the recording is zero-padded to double length. **CONFIRMED.** RoomScope: use linear (zero-padded) deconvolution and window the IR from just before the direct sound; discard everything earlier than the 2nd-harmonic lead Δt2.
- **Sweep duration / silence (2000 Sec. 3; Müller & Massarani Sec. 1.x–2.1):** use a single long sweep rather than averaging (time-variance robustness, S/N grows with T); add a silence after the sweep at least as long as the reverberation (at the highest frequencies) so the tail is captured; the capture period must be at least as long as the IR.
- **Synchronisation (Novak et al. 2015):** if harmonic IRs are to be analysed, choose T so that L = T/ln(f2/f1) makes f1·L an integer (their "synchronized" ESS) — optional for RoomScope.

### B.5 Fade-in/out and frequency-range extension (ripple / pre-ringing)

*Sources:* Farina 2007 §3.1 (pages 5–8 of the paper); Müller & Massarani 2001 (Sec. 1.4 TDS ripple discussion, and Sec. 4.2 "sweeps created in the spectral domain").

- **Müller & Massarani:** an abruptly switched sine has a corrugated (sinc-convolved) spectrum near the start frequency; "a common way to circumvent this problem is to let the excitation sweep start well below the lowest frequency of interest" (and, symmetrically, extend beyond the highest). "To force the sweep's desired start and end point to zero, fade operations of the first half-wave and the tail are indispensable to avoid switching noise"; **half-cosine windows** are most suitable; keep the faded portions short so the ripple stays **< 0.1 dB**, and it is cancelled anyway by the reference (loopback) measurement. Their spectral-domain synthesis (group-delay design) avoids leakage entirely; a simple iteration (fade → FFT → impose target magnitude → IFFT → repeat) converges in ~15 iterations.
- **Farina 2007:** band-limiting (e.g. 22 Hz–22 kHz) plus 0.1 s fade-in/out on a 15 s ESS forms a trapezoidal spectral window ⇒ sinc-like **pre-ringing** around the direct sound. Removing the **fade-out** removes most HF pre-ringing, but a sweep must not end on a non-zero value (step ⇒ broadband click): instead **sweep up to the Nyquist frequency and cut at the last zero-crossing**. Removing the fade-in does *not* help LF pre-ringing, which comes from the analogue chain; cure it with a Kirkeby regularised inverse ("packing") filter C(f) = H*(f)/(H*(f)H(f) + ε(f)), with ε(f) small inside [f_low, f_high] and large outside. Farina's conclusion: "pre-ringing artifacts can be substantially avoided by combining the usage of a wide-band sweep running up to the Nyquist frequency, without any fade-out, and ... a suitable compacting inverse filter".
- **RoomScope defaults derived from the above:** sweep from ≈ 10–20 Hz (below the lowest band of interest) to fs/2 (or at least 1 octave above the highest band); short (≈ 20–100 ms, half-cosine) fade-in; either very short fade-out ending at a zero crossing or none; silence ≥ expected RT; optional loopback reference to divide out the interface response.

### B.6 Clock mismatch and time variance (Farina 2007 §3.4; Müller & Massarani)

Playback/record clocks need not be locked; small mismatch leaves the IR clean, large mismatch "skews" the IR in the time–frequency plane (visible as a smeared HF direct sound). Use one full-duplex interface where possible; otherwise estimate the drift from a loopback or from the harmonic-line slope and resample. (Note the Saint-Gobain patent US 10,816,391 B2 in Part C claims a specific drift-correction scheme between *separate, unsynchronised* devices.)

### B.7 Background noise reporting without SPL calibration

- Without a calibrated microphone chain there is no path to dB SPL, NR/NC or LAeq; report levels **in dB FS per AES17-2020** (0 dB FS = RMS of a full-scale sine). State the reference explicitly ("RMS, sine-referenced, AES17"), and give both broadband and per-band values so a user with a known mic sensitivity/preamp gain can convert later. **CONFIRMED** (AES17 definition, A.6/18).
- Hum detection: report the level of spectral peaks at 50/60 Hz and harmonics relative to the surrounding noise floor (dB re noise floor) and relative to full scale (dB FS). No standard defines this analysis; it is straightforward spectral peak detection (Welch PSD) and needs no citation beyond the PSD method (Welch 1967, IEEE Trans. Audio Electroacoust. 15(2):70–73, DOI 10.1109/TAU.1967.1161901 — DOI not re-verified in this session).
- ISO 3382-2 requires the background noise to be measured (for the 10 dB / 35–45 dB rules) — in RoomScope this is the noise floor of the *deconvolved* IR per band (Lundeby estimate) plus the raw recording's noise, both in dB FS.

### B.8 Image-source picture (visualization only)

pyroomacoustics documents `Room.plot` as plotting "the room with its walls, microphones, sources and images" (https://pyroomacoustics.readthedocs.io/en/pypi-release/pyroomacoustics.room.html). RoomScope's placement picture already showed the microphone, one cabinet and the known planes. It now also draws the first-order image through the upper plane and the specular bounce (`horizontal_plane_image_path`), from Allen & Berkley 1979 [20]. No wall is invented. The pyroomacoustics page is the idea source; **no source was copied.**

### B.9 External lidar / mesh scan (file import only)

RoomScope does not talk to a lidar. A user who already has a scan can import ASCII PLY (Stanford Triangle Format, Greg Turk; http://paulbourke.net/dataformats/ply/) or Wavefront OBJ (Library of Congress FDD000507). The readers in `io/scan.py` follow those public layouts only; binary PLY is refused. Coordinates are file units treated as metres and are drawn as an overlay. **No Open3D, trimesh or CloudCompare source was copied.** The checked-in fixture is a synthetic shoebox, not a capture.

### B.10 Impulse-response spectrum

The frequency-response tab is a gated FFT of a window around the direct sound. The noise tab is a Welch PSD of a quiet recording segment. Alongside those, RoomScope now reports a Welch (1967) PSD of the deconvolved IR itself (`core/spectrum.py`), with the same AES17 density scaling as B.7 (`10*log10(2*psd)`). SciPy's public `welch` is used; no third-party measurement program's spectrum code was copied. This VM shows it on synthetic recordings and the fake backend, not on a physical analyser.

### B.11 Follow the DAW in play (no host query)

The sweep must be generated at the project sample rate (daw-setup.md). RoomScope already treated that rate as DAW-dependent; it does not copy export bit depth or time-stretch from a host. `daw.py` resolves a named or fake project and asks when none or several are in play. This VM has no DAW; the path is `ROOMSCOPE_FAKE_DAWS` / injected entries only. No DAW SDK and no process scan.

### C.3 Practical conclusions (not legal advice)

1. Generating a log sine sweep, deconvolving with the inverse filter, Schroeder integration, ISO 3382 EDT/T20/T30 with Lundeby truncation, fractional-octave filtering, ETC-based reflection detection and decay-time-vs-frequency mode detection are all documented in publications dated 1965–2003 and in ISO 3382 / ISO 18233; no in-force patent found claims any of them as such.
2. Two in-force patents deserve a note in the design docs so the implementation does not drift into them: **US 9,959,883 B2** (automatic two-sweep pass-band scheme) and **US 10,816,391 B2** (drift correction between unsynchronised emitter/receiver devices). Neither affects the baseline single-sweep, single-interface design.
3. Room-correction filter design is densely patented (Dirac, Sonarworks, Audyssey/Sound United, DTS, Harman, Genelec-expired). RoomScope should keep stating that it measures and reports only.
