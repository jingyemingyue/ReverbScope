Measure what matters

A room acoustics measurement tool built around one practical question: is this microphone position usable?

📡

Exponential sine sweep

Probe the room with a log sine sweep and derive the room impulse response by deconvolution — the same method the pros use.

⏱️

RT60 · EDT · T20 · T30

Reverberation time from the decay curve, each value carrying its decay range and a validity flag.

🌊

Early reflections

Reflection timing and level relative to the direct sound, so you can see what the room adds to the microphone.

🔇

Background noise

Noise-floor analysis with mains-hum detection — know your room's quietest moment.

🎯

C50 · C80 · D50

ISO 3382-1 clarity, definition and centre time for speech and music. Labelled recording profiles interpret them for vocals, drums, voiceover and more.

🎛️

DAW-independent

Standalone mode measures through your interface directly; Universal DAW Mode works with any DAW that exports WAV. No DAW SDKs, ever.

🌐

English / 简体中文

GUI, command line and reports fully bilingual — the same measurement, in your language.

🛡️

Honest numbers

Every metric carries its unit, algorithm source and validity flag. When the data is not good enough, ReverbScope says insufficient range instead of inventing a figure. No pseudo-scientific room score.

FAQ

Is ReverbScope a free REW alternative?

Yes — ReverbScope is a free, open-source alternative to Room EQ Wizard (REW) for room acoustics measurement. It measures reverberation time (RT60), early reflections, background noise and clarity metrics (C50/C80/D50) from an exponential sine sweep, runs next to any DAW instead of inside it, and costs nothing: Apache-2.0 licensed, no paid tiers, no feature gates.

What is room acoustics measurement, and what does RT60 mean?

Room acoustics measurement characterizes how a room changes sound. ReverbScope plays an exponential sine sweep, derives the room impulse response, and reports reverberation time — RT60 (the time for sound to decay 60 dB), plus EDT, T20 and T30 — along with early reflections, frequency response and background noise.

Do I need a DAW or special hardware?

No. In standalone mode ReverbScope plays and records through your audio interface itself — all you need is a microphone. In Universal DAW Mode you can play the sweep through any DAW that imports, plays, records and exports WAV files.

How is ReverbScope different from other analyzers?

Three things: it is DAW-independent by design (WAV in, WAV out, no DAW SDKs); it refuses to invent a number (validity flags on every metric, no room score); and it answers the practical question — is this position usable for a vocal, drums, or voiceover? — through labelled recording profiles instead of raw graphs alone.
